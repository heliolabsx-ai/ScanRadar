## What was missing
1. **A regime read.** Nothing in the system said "it is a bear day, stop judging longs". Breadth was on the page from 09:35 and no rule used it.
2. **Scan-owned stops.** Alert stops/targets come from pivots and order blocks (`side_levels`), not from the scan's own logic, so R:R is meaningless and the desk skipped on it. Bounce failure's natural stop is the bar-2 high.
3. **Budget allocation.** 60 % of the desk's money went to rule alerts that fire 300–500 times a day; pattern triggers and the 15-minute briefs were starved.
4. **Fire caps.** Two scans produced 1,015 fires. A scan that fires on every name every 30 minutes is a filter, not a signal.

## What filters the noise (in order of value)
1. Breadth gate: when bears > 60 % of the universe, longs (scans, patterns, desk) go to "watch only"; mirror for bulls. One number, already computed every minute.
2. Scans define stop/target: `"stop": {"off": 2, "field": "high"}` and a target rule in the JSON; levels follow the scan's logic; R:R becomes real; the backtest grades the real trade.
3. Per-scan hour-of-day and confidence record on the alert, like the Conf column on patterns; the desk and Telegram only below/above a floor.
4. Desk budget by value: pattern triggers first (they have a recorded confidence), then alerts with R:R ≥ 1.5 from scans whose record clears a floor, then briefs; alerts share 30 %, not 60 %.
5. Cap fires: per scan per hour (e.g. 10), ranked by R:R, and a 60-minute cooldown on the two high-volume scans.
6. Kill or redefine the weak shapes: bull/bear flags and triangles as defined have 12–23 % hit rates over 42 days; keep them off the desk (already) or require the breakout to come with the day's direction.
